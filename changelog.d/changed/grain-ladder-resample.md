- **Grain ladder re-measured at 9x the sample — several published figures were wrong.**
  The first profile used 86 titles at one scene each; it is now **752 titles across 2230
  scenes** (418 films + 334 episodes, three scenes per title, per-title median). Individual
  decade buckets moved by up to **78%**, the overall median by 38% (0.0067 -> 0.0048), and
  two conclusions were simply false: the 1960s are *not* the grainiest decade (mid-pack;
  the 1970s-80s are the peak) and TV does *not* stay grainier than film into the 2010s
  (they converge). Quantified why: the within-title scene-to-scene spread has a median of
  **0.71x the title's own median**, so a single-scene probe typically misestimates by ~36%
  and for a tenth of titles by 100%+. New finding invisible at the smaller n: **the 1080p
  tier is 2.2x grainier than the 4K tier** (median 0.0072 vs 0.0032, like-for-like native
  crops) — 4K masters are denoised hard enough to compress, so 1080p is where the removable
  impairment actually lives. See [docs/development/grain-ladder.md](docs/development/grain-ladder.md).
