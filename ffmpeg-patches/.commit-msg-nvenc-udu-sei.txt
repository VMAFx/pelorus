fix(nvenc): write Pelorus side data zero-free and within NVENC's header limit

NVENC has two defects that hit every user data unregistered SEI with
udu_sei=1:

1. hevc_nvenc fails a picture whose non-VCL NAL units (VPS, SPS, PPS,
access unit delimiter and every SEI NAL unit, in Annex B form) exceed
1024 bytes. FFmpeg drops a SEI that takes the payload bytes of a picture
above 512 (NVENC_HEVC_SEI_MAX_PAYLOAD); the shared FFmpeg fix series
(VMAFx/ffmpeg-patches, ADR-0185) carried a NAL size budget of 768 bytes
for the same defect before that.

2. h264_nvenc and hevc_nvenc write a SEI payload truncated, without its
trailing bits, when its emulation prevention bytes exceed ceil(P / 3) + 3
for a P-byte payload (rule pinned on an RTX 4090, driver 615.78.08), and
FFmpeg's decoders then drop it.

Both would lose Pelorus side data. The zero runs of a
pelorus_analyze_vulkan blob on flat content reach rule 2: at 1080p with
32-pixel cells (12 424 bytes) no picture kept its side data. At real
frame sizes the analyze maps do not fit rule 1.

This patch adds one call right after the av_memdup() of each
SEI_UNREGISTERED entry. It needs nothing from the series and applies
with or without it:

- Every Pelorus blob (UUID e1d7c4a2-6b93-4f08-9a55-0f3c2db17e64, magic
  PELOR1, ABI major 1) is replaced by its zero-free carrier form of the
  Pelorus interop ABI 1.5: UUID 3f9b37b8-fd9a-4621-920e-9b78b55cf9b5,
  then the COBS encoding of the blob image. It holds no zero byte, so
  its NAL unit needs no emulation prevention and is never truncated;
  it costs one byte plus one per 254 bytes. libpelorus's
  pel_blob_unwrap() turns it back into the blob. Any other payload is
  left as copied.
- On hevc_nvenc, the SEI before the blob in the picture are charged
  against two limits Pelorus keeps itself: 768 NAL bytes (1024 less a
  256-byte reserve for the NAL units NVENC writes on its own) and 512
  payload bytes, FFmpeg's limit. A blob whose carrier does not fit what
  is left first loses the per-cell maps appended after its sections.
  Every scalar stays, and each map offset and size is zero, which the
  Pelorus ABI defines as an absent map. The blob is byte-identical to the
  analyze filter's maps=0 blob before it becomes a carrier. A carrier
  that still does not fit is dropped here, so FFmpeg's own check never
  drops a Pelorus blob.

The maps are stripped in the copy, and the carrier is written into one
new allocation that replaces it. The first stripped or dropped blob is a
warning, later ones verbose, and the totals are a warning when the
encoder closes; the first carrier is noted in the verbose log. The size
arithmetic, the stripper and the carrier encoder live in
libavcodec/pelorus_sei_fit.h,
which the Pelorus fast suite tests against the libpelorus packer and
carrier encoder.

Measured on an RTX 4090, driver 615.71.09 and 615.78.08: the largest
payload that encodes is 898 bytes on an IDR picture and 999 bytes on a P
picture at 1080p, which is 1024 bytes of non-VCL NAL units in both cases.
48 analyze blobs (320x180 to 1920x1080, flat, gradient and detailed
content, maps on and off): 11 of 48 lost every picture's side data on
h264_nvenc as written before, none as carriers, up to 49 145 bytes.
