fix(nvenc): write Pelorus side data zero-free and within NVENC's header limit

Two NVENC defects lose user data unregistered SEI that Pelorus filters
attach to frames with udu_sei=1.

1. hevc_nvenc stops the encode with "Failed locking bitstream buffer: out
of memory" (AVERROR(ENOMEM)) once a picture's SEI is large. NVENC's HEVC
encoder accepts at most 1024 bytes of non-VCL NAL units per picture: VPS,
SPS, PPS, access unit delimiter and every SEI NAL unit, in Annex B form
with start codes and emulation prevention bytes. Above that,
nvEncLockBitstream() returns NV_ENC_ERR_OUT_OF_MEMORY. At the end of a
stream the same picture is lost with only an error line. h264_nvenc
carries 64 KiB payloads. Neither nvEncodeAPI.h 13.1 nor the NVENC
programming guide 13.1 states the limit.

2. h264_nvenc and hevc_nvenc write a SEI payload truncated, without its
trailing bits, when its emulation prevention bytes exceed ceil(P / 3) + 3
for a P-byte payload (rule pinned on an RTX 4090, driver 615.78.08);
FFmpeg's decoders then drop it without an error.
The zero runs of a pelorus_analyze_vulkan blob on flat content reach
that: at 1080p with 32-pixel cells (12 424 bytes) no picture kept its
side data.

Every Pelorus blob (UUID e1d7c4a2-6b93-4f08-9a55-0f3c2db17e64, magic
PELOR1, ABI major 1) is written in the zero-free carrier form of the
Pelorus interop ABI 1.5: UUID 3f9b37b8-fd9a-4621-920e-9b78b55cf9b5, then
the COBS encoding of the blob image. It holds no zero byte, so its NAL
unit needs no emulation prevention, and costs one byte plus one per 254
bytes. libpelorus's pel_blob_unwrap() turns it back into the blob. Any
other payload is written unchanged. The carrier is encoded straight from
the side data into the payload's one allocation, as the stock
av_memdup() did; a blob that loses its maps is stripped in a per-encoder
buffer reused across frames and freed at close. The first carrier is
noted in the verbose log.

For HEVC, budget 768 bytes per picture for the SEI passed in
seiPayloadArray, less the A/53 and timecode SEI already queued, and keep
256 bytes for the NAL units NVENC writes on its own (128 bytes measured at
8K Main10 with VUI and AUD). Each SEI_UNREGISTERED entry, in frame order,
is charged as written (a Pelorus blob as its carrier):

- fits: written whole;
- a Pelorus blob that does not fit: written without the per-cell maps
  appended after its sections. Every scalar stays, and each map offset and
  size is zero, which the Pelorus ABI defines as an absent map. The blob
  is byte-identical to the analyze filter's maps=0 blob before it becomes
  a carrier;
- anything else that does not fit: not written.

The first stripped blob and the first dropped payload are warnings, later
ones verbose, and the totals are a warning when the encoder closes. The
size arithmetic and the carrier encoder live in
libavcodec/pelorus_sei_fit.h, which the Pelorus fast suite tests against
the libpelorus packer and carrier encoder.

Measured on an RTX 4090, driver 615.71.09 and 615.78.08: the largest
payload that encodes is 898 bytes on an IDR picture and 999 bytes on a P
picture at 1080p, which is 1024 bytes of non-VCL NAL units in both cases.
48 analyze blobs (320x180 to 1920x1080, flat, gradient and detailed
content, maps on and off): 11 of 48 lost every picture's side data on
h264_nvenc as written before, none as carriers, up to 49 145 bytes.
